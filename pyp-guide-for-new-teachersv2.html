01 · Start here

What the PYP is

The Primary Years Programme is a curriculum framework from the International Baccalaureate (IB) for students aged 3 to 12. It doesn't tell you exactly what to teach each day — it gives you a structured, research-informed way to organise learning around big ideas, real inquiry, and the development of the whole child.

◆Big Idea

The PYP is a framework, not a fixed syllabus. It defines how learning is structured and what kind of learner you're growing, while leaving schools and teachers significant freedom over the specific content.

Who it's for

Ages 3–12

Early years through the end of primary school. Any school teaching this age range can apply to become an IB World School offering the PYP.

The shape of it

Transdisciplinary

Learning is organised around six themes that cut across traditional subjects, so children see how knowledge connects in the real world.

The engine

Inquiry-based

Students build understanding by questioning, investigating, and constructing meaning — guided by the teacher, not simply told.

→Teacher Translation

If you're coming from a content-driven system, the biggest shift is this: your job moves from delivering information toward designing experiences in which children investigate meaningful questions and build understanding they can transfer to new situations.

The PYP is the first programme in the IB continuum, which continues into the Middle Years Programme (MYP), the Diploma Programme (DP), and the Career-related Programme (CP). It is not a prerequisite for the later programmes, but it sets up the same values: inquiry, international-mindedness, and learning that matters beyond the classroom.

02 · The why

The purpose & philosophy of the PYP

At its heart, the PYP aims to develop internationally-minded people who, recognising their common humanity and shared guardianship of the planet, help to create a better and more peaceful world.

That mission shows up in the classroom as a focus on the whole child — intellectual, social, emotional, and physical growth — rather than test scores alone. The PYP is built on constructivist and social-constructivist ideas: learners actively build understanding, and they do it best in dialogue with others.

What it values

Education as engaged & significant

Learning should be engaging, relevant, challenging, and significant. Children explore ideas that genuinely matter to them and to the world.

What it produces

Knowledge plus dispositions

Students develop knowledge, conceptual understanding, skills, and the attributes of the Learner Profile — and use them to make a difference.

iKey phrase you'll hear constantly

International-mindedness. It doesn't mean "learning about other countries." It means developing the dispositions — curiosity, open-mindedness, care — to understand multiple perspectives and act responsibly in an interconnected world.

03 · The model

The learner at the centre

The PYP curriculum framework rests on three pillars, with agency threaded through all of them. Together they answer three questions: what is learning, how is it best supported, and who makes it happen?

The Learner

The outcomes for individual students — and the outcomes they seek for themselves.

"What is learning?"

Learning & Teaching

The distinctive features of how learning happens and how teachers support it.

"How best to support learners?"

The Learning Community

The social side of learning and the role of the whole community — peers, families, staff.

"Who facilitates learning and teaching?"

Agency & self-efficacy run through all three pillars The learner is an agent of their own — and others' — learning.

Within "the learner" sit the framework's essential elements — the things every unit is built from: the Learner Profile (who we're becoming), knowledge (organised through the transdisciplinary themes), conceptual understanding (the big ideas), Approaches to Learning (transferable skills), and action (learning that leads to doing). Each of these has its own section below.

04 · The heartbeat

Agency, voice, choice & ownership

Agency is the idea that students are partners in learning, not passive recipients of it. It is arguably the central principle of the modern PYP — and the one new teachers most often underestimate.

Voice

Students share ideas, ask questions, and influence what and how they learn. Their thinking is visible and taken seriously.

Choice

Students make real decisions — about how to investigate, how to show learning, and where an inquiry should go next.

Ownership

Students set goals, monitor their progress, and take responsibility for their learning and its consequences.

→Teacher Translation

Agency is not "the children do whatever they want." It's intentional structure that creates space for genuine decision-making. You still plan rigorously — you plan for choice rather than removing it.

Closely linked is self-efficacy: a learner's belief that their efforts make a difference. Students with strong self-efficacy take initiative in their learning and take action in their community. Building it is part of your daily work — through trust, feedback, and authentic responsibility.

05 · Who we're growing

The IB Learner Profile

The Learner Profile is a set of ten attributes that describe the kind of person the whole IB continuum aims to develop. It's the shared language of every IB World School, from age 3 to 18, and it belongs to everyone — students, teachers, leaders, and families.

InquirersCurious; develop skills for inquiry and lifelong learning.
KnowledgeableExplore ideas of local and global significance.
ThinkersThink critically and creatively to solve problems.
CommunicatorsExpress ideas confidently in many ways and languages.
PrincipledAct with integrity, honesty, fairness, and respect.
Open-mindedValue other perspectives, cultures, and traditions.
CaringShow empathy, compassion, and a commitment to service.
Risk-takers (Courageous)Approach the unfamiliar with forethought and resilience.
BalancedAttend to intellectual, physical, and emotional wellbeing.
ReflectiveConsider their learning and experience to grow.
✦Classroom Example

Rather than displaying all ten as posters and moving on, a teacher names attributes as they happen: "You kept going when that was hard — that's being a risk-taker." Naming the behaviour in the moment is what makes the profile real for young learners.

The Learner Profile is developmental, not a checklist or a behaviour-reward chart. Children grow into the attributes over years; teacher modelling matters as much as student practice.

06 · The engine

Inquiry-based learning

Inquiry is the PYP's main approach to learning. Students build understanding by wondering, investigating, and making sense of things — guided by a skilled teacher who designs the conditions for thinking.

Inquiry sits on a spectrum. Sometimes it's structured (teacher-framed question and method), sometimes guided (students choose the path within a shared focus), and sometimes open (students drive question and method). Skilled PYP teachers move fluidly along this spectrum depending on the goal and the learners.

A common inquiry rhythm

Many PYP schools use a cycle like the one below to shape a unit. The phases are a widely-used model, not fixed IB terminology — your school may name them differently.

→Teacher Translation

Inquiry is not "no teaching." You teach skills, model thinking, give direct instruction when it serves the inquiry, and provide carefully chosen provocations and resources. The difference is that the learning is built with students rather than simply handed to them.

07 · How learning connects

Transdisciplinary learning

"Transdisciplinary" means learning that rises above and connects across individual subjects, organised around themes of genuine significance. Subjects still matter — but they serve a bigger conceptual purpose.

Three ways subjects can relate — only the third is transdisciplinary
ApproachWhat it looks likeWhere meaning lives
DisciplinaryMaths, then reading, then science — taught separately.Inside each subject.
MultidisciplinaryA shared topic ("Victorians") with separate subject activities loosely linked by it.Still mostly inside each subject; the link is surface-level.
TransdisciplinaryA conceptual central idea that subjects genuinely combine to explore.In the big idea — subjects are tools for understanding it.
◆Big Idea

In a transdisciplinary unit, you don't ask "what maths can I bolt onto this topic?" You ask "what understanding are we building, and which subjects help us get there?" The concept comes first; the subjects follow.

PYP students still develop strong subject knowledge and skills — in language, mathematics, the arts, science, social studies, and physical/personal/social education. Some learning is taught within units of inquiry; some (like aspects of maths and language) is taught alongside them and connected wherever it's meaningful, not forced.

08 · The organising frame

The six transdisciplinary themes

Six themes give the whole programme its shape. Each year, classes explore units under these themes, building a school-wide Programme of Inquiry. The themes are the same across every PYP school in the world.

Who we are

Identity; beliefs and values; health and relationships; what it means to be human — including our connection to other living things.

Plain English: Who am I, and how do I relate to others and the world?

Where we are in place & time

Orientation in place and time; personal histories; journeys, discoveries, and the relationships between people and environments over time.

Plain English: Where do we come from, and how has that shaped us?

How we express ourselves

The ways we discover and express ideas, feelings, culture, beliefs, and creativity — across many forms and media.

Plain English: How do we communicate and create meaning?

How the world works

The natural world and its laws; the interaction of the natural and human-made; how scientific principles shape our lives and environments.

Plain English: How do natural and physical systems work?

How we organize ourselves

Human-made systems and communities; how organisations work; decision-making; economic activity and its impact on people and the environment.

Plain English: How do people structure and run their societies?

Sharing the planet

Rights and responsibilities in a shared world; communities and relationships within and between them; access to equal opportunities; peace and conflict resolution.

Plain English: How do we live well together and with the planet?

↻Recently updated

The six theme names are unchanged, but the IB published new theme descriptors in December 2024. The descriptors now use a short driving statement plus three conceptual support statements, and rebalance the focus from "commonalities of human experience" toward the interconnectedness of the human and natural worlds. The summaries above reflect that direction. For the exact official wording, use the descriptors on the Programme Resource Centre / My IB. Schools have until September 2027 to fully transition.

09 · The depth

Concepts & conceptual understanding

Concepts are the big, transferable ideas that lift learning above isolated facts. They're what lets a child take an understanding built in one unit and apply it somewhere completely new.

Facts
"A frog lays eggs in water." Specific, useful, but tied to one case.
Topic
"Life cycles of animals." Organises related facts.
Concept
Change. A transferable lens that applies far beyond frogs.
Understanding
"Living things change in predictable stages over time." A durable idea students can transfer.

The seven key (specified) concepts

Each is best remembered through the question it prompts students to ask.

Form

What is it like?

Function

How does it work?

Causation

Why is it like it is?

Change

How is it transforming?

Connection

How is it linked to other things?

Perspective

What are the points of view?

Responsibility

What is our responsibility?

↻Terminology note (emerging)

Under the new subject guidance, you'll increasingly see the seven concepts referred to as "specified concepts," and the more subject-specific ideas attached to a unit (previously called related concepts) referred to as "additional concepts." The underlying meanings are unchanged. Confirm the exact terminology your school is adopting against official IB guidance, as this is part of the ongoing refresh.

→Teacher Translation

When planning, ask: "If students forget the facts in five years, what idea do I want to stick?" That idea, expressed conceptually, is the spine of your unit's central idea.

10 · The skills

Approaches to Learning (ATL)

Approaches to Learning are the transferable skills students develop through their learning — the "how" of being a capable, independent learner. They're grouped into five categories and run through the whole IB continuum.

Thinking skills

Critical and creative thinking, transfer, reflection, metacognition.

Research skills

Formulating questions, locating and evaluating information, using media ethically.

Communication skills

Listening, speaking, reading, writing, and using multiple modes and languages.

Social skills

Collaboration, relationships, resolving conflict, making group decisions.

Self-management skills

Organisation, time and task management, states of mind, perseverance.

✦Classroom Example

A unit on community decisions might explicitly teach the social skill of reaching group consensus — modelled, practised, and reflected on — alongside the content. ATL skills are taught on purpose, not assumed.

11 · Learning that does

Action in the PYP

Action is the bridge between understanding and doing. When learning is meaningful, students are moved to do something with it — and that is treated as a natural, expected outcome of inquiry, not an add-on.

The cycle of action

Reflect → Choose → Act ↺

Students reflect on what they understand, choose a response, act — and the cycle continues as they reflect again.

Action takes many forms

Participation

Engaging actively in their own class, school, or community life.

Advocacy

Speaking up to support a cause or a change they believe in.

Social justice

Acting on fairness, rights, and equity.

Social entrepreneurship

Creating innovative responses to community needs.

Lifestyle choices

Changing personal habits and behaviours.

iImportant

Action is student-initiated and authentic. A small, genuine action a child chooses themselves matters more than a large, teacher-imposed "project." Sometimes action is quiet and internal — a changed mind is action too.

12 · Knowing where learners are

Assessment in the PYP

PYP assessment is about understanding and improving learning, not ranking children. There are no external standardised PYP exams; teachers gather rich evidence to inform teaching and to help students grow.

Before & during

Assessment for learning

Formative checks that shape what happens next — the everyday engine of teaching.

Through reflection

Assessment as learning

Students self- and peer-assess, building metacognition and ownership.

At key points

Assessment of learning

Summative evidence of understanding at the end of a phase or unit.

Good PYP assessment is built into the unit from the start. Teachers and students often co-construct success criteria, gather evidence in portfolios, and use clear, kind, actionable feedback. The four dimensions teachers attend to are monitoring, documenting, measuring, and reporting learning.

→Teacher Translation

You'll likely use fewer one-off graded tests and more ongoing evidence — observations, conversations, student work, and reflections — to build a picture of each learner over time. Plan how you'll capture evidence before the unit starts.

13 · The culmination

The Exhibition (PYPX)

In the final year of the PYP (typically around age 11–12), students undertake the Exhibition — an extended, collaborative inquiry that brings together everything the programme stands for. It's a milestone, not a test.

What students do

Working in groups, students identify a real issue, plan and carry out an in-depth inquiry, take genuine action, and present their learning to the school community.

What it demonstrates

It shows students can drive their own learning, apply the essential elements (concepts, skills, the Learner Profile), and act with agency and responsibility.

iGood to know

The Exhibition is student-led; teachers and mentors guide and support rather than direct. It is often (but not required to be) explored through the Sharing the planet theme, and frequently involves families and community members as audience and contributors.

14 · How it's built

Planning & collaboration

The PYP is a collaborative programme. Teachers plan together, document with a shared planner, and map learning across the whole school so children experience a coherent, balanced journey.

The Programme of Inquiry (POI)

The POI is the school's master map: it shows which units (under which of the six themes) each year group explores. In the primary years, classes typically explore up to six units of inquiry per year — one per theme — while the early years are more flexible. The POI ensures balance and avoids repetition across grades.

Anatomy of a Unit of Inquiry (UOI)

Central idea
One enduring, conceptual statement that anchors the whole unit.
Transdisciplinary theme

Which of the six themes frames the unit.

Key concepts

The conceptual lenses students explore.

Lines of inquiry

2–4 focus areas that unpack the central idea.

ATL skills

Transferable skills taught and practised.

Learner Profile

Attributes intentionally developed.

Action & assessment

How learning is shown and acted upon.

→Teacher Translation

You will rarely plan alone. Expect regular collaborative planning meetings with your grade team and specialists. The shared planner is a living document you revisit and revise as the inquiry unfolds — not a form you fill in once.

Your school operates within the IB's Programme Standards and Practices, the requirements every IB World School is evaluated against. Your PYP Coordinator is your go-to person for how these apply locally.

15 · On a Monday morning

Here's how the philosophy translates into the daily texture of a PYP classroom — the practical shifts most new teachers notice first.

You might be used to…In the PYP, lean toward…
Starting with the answer/contentStarting with a provocation that sparks questions
Covering a topicUncovering a concept students can transfer
Teacher sets all goalsStudents help set goals and success criteria
Subjects taught in silosSubjects connected through a central idea where meaningful

iFor specialists, TAs & leaders

Specialist teachers (PE, arts, languages) connect to units where authentic — and teach essential disciplinary skills that stand on their own. Teaching assistants are part of the learning community, supporting inquiry, documenting learning, and modelling the Learner Profile. Leaders protect collaborative planning time and nurture the culture of agency.

16 · See it in action

Brief classroom examples

One short, illustrative example for each major PYP idea. These are typical of PYP practice — not official IB units — to help you picture how the pieces show up.

Inquiry

Following the wonder

A puddle on the playground becomes a week of investigation into evaporation, weather, and measurement — sparked by children's own questions.

Transdisciplinary

Markets & maths

A unit on local communities ("How we organize ourselves") uses real maths (money, data), language (interviews), and social studies together.

Concept

"Change" travels

Having explored change in life cycles, students spot it again in seasons, friendships, and stories — and name the connection themselves.

Agency

Choosing the path

Within a shared central idea, groups choose which sub-question to pursue and how to present findings — a poster, a model, or a short film.

Learner Profile

Named in the moment

"You disagreed respectfully and listened — that's being open-minded," a teacher notes during a debate, making the attribute concrete.

ATL skills

Teaching collaboration

Before group work, the class co-builds a "what good collaboration looks like" chart, then reflects against it afterward.

Action

Small but real

After a unit on water, a child starts turning off taps and persuades their family — a genuine lifestyle-choice action they chose.

Assessment

Evidence over time

A student's portfolio shows three drafts of an explanation, with their own reflections on how their thinking changed.

Exhibition

A community issue

A final-year group investigates food waste in the canteen, surveys peers, proposes a plan, and presents it to staff and families.

17 · Clearing the air

Common misconceptions for new PYP teachers

A few honest corrections that save new teachers a lot of early confusion.

Inquiry means I don't teach.
You teach a great deal — skills, knowledge, and thinking — and use direct instruction when it serves the inquiry. You design learning with students rather than simply transmitting it.
Agency means children do whatever they want.
Agency is structured and intentional. You plan carefully to create real, bounded choices — not to remove guidance.
The PYP tells me exactly what to teach.
It's a framework, not a fixed syllabus. It defines the structure and the kind of learner; schools and teachers shape much of the content.
Everything must be transdisciplinary.
No. Core skills (much of maths and language) are taught directly and connected to units only where it's authentic. Forced links weaken learning.
The Learner Profile is a behaviour chart.
It's a developmental set of dispositions, not a reward-and-punishment system. It's named and modelled, not scored.
Action has to be big and visible.
Authentic, student-chosen action — however small or quiet — matters more than large teacher-driven projects.
No tests means no assessment.
Assessment is constant and rich; it's just more formative and evidence-based than a single graded exam.
I have to plan it all alone.
Collaboration is core to the PYP. You plan with your team, and your coordinator supports you.
18 · Speak the language

Key terminology glossary

The essential PYP vocabulary, in plain terms. Tap any term to expand.

Agency +
The capacity and right of learners to take an active role in their learning — through voice, choice, and ownership.
Approaches to Learning (ATL) +
Transferable skills in five categories: thinking, research, communication, social, and self-management.
Central idea +
The single, enduring, conceptual statement that anchors a unit of inquiry.
Essential elements +
The building blocks of every unit: the Learner Profile, knowledge, conceptual understanding, ATL skills, and action.
Exhibition (PYPX) +
The collaborative, student-led culminating inquiry in the final year of the PYP.
International-mindedness +
The disposition to understand multiple perspectives and act responsibly in an interconnected world; the overarching aim of the programme.
Key / specified concepts +
The seven transferable concepts — form, function, causation, change, connection, perspective, responsibility. Increasingly termed "specified concepts" under new guidance.
Additional / related concepts +
More subject-specific concepts attached to a particular unit. "Related concepts" is the established term; "additional concepts" is the emerging term.
Lines of inquiry +
The two to four focus areas that unpack and structure a central idea.
Learner Profile +
The ten attributes describing the kind of learner the IB aims to develop, shared across all IB programmes.
Programme of Inquiry (POI) +
The whole-school map of which units are taught under each theme in each year group.
Provocation +
A stimulus — object, image, question, experience — used to spark curiosity and surface thinking at the start of inquiry.
Subject continuum +
The newer structure for subject learning progression, replacing the previous "scope and sequence" documents as part of the refresh.
Transdisciplinary themes +
The six globally significant themes that organise the programme of inquiry.
Unit of Inquiry (UOI) +
A single inquiry unit built around a central idea and a transdisciplinary theme.
Programme Standards & Practices +
The IB's requirements that every IB World School is evaluated against.
19 · Looking ahead

PYP 2026 / upcoming changes

The IB is rolling out a multi-year curriculum refresh for the PYP. It is evolution, not reinvention — the framework's foundations (the three pillars, agency, inquiry, the six themes, the Learner Profile) remain. Below is what's changing and when, with emerging details flagged for you to confirm officially.

December 2024

New transdisciplinary theme descriptors published on the Programme Resource Centre, with supporting teacher resources for transition.

2025 (rolling)

New subject guidance begins replacing the old "scope and sequence" with subject continuums; learning progressions for inquiry, additional-language resources, early-years resources, and curriculum-design tools are released.

From 2025

A new workshop, "Building your IB programme: Primary years," becomes available to support all PYP teachers with the updated guidance and conceptual understanding.

By September 2027

Full transition required. Schools may adopt the updates at their own pace until this point, when the new guidance becomes fully expected.

What's actually changing

Themes

Refreshed descriptors

Same six theme names; new wording that rebalances toward the interconnectedness of human and natural worlds.

Concepts

Clearer language

Terminology shifting toward "specified" and "additional" concepts. The seven concepts and their meanings stay the same.

Subjects

Scope & sequence → continuums

A more concept-driven, connected way of describing progression across subjects.

Inquiry

Inquiry learning progressions

New tools to make growth in inquiry more visible and trackable, supporting agency.

↻To be confirmed by official IB guidance

"PYP 2026" is not a single official product name — it's shorthand many schools use for this refresh. Exact terminology, document names, and required practices are still settling and may vary by region and rollout phase. Always verify specifics against the current documents on My IB and the Programme Resource Centre, and check with your PYP Coordinator about your school's transition plan. Treat third-party summaries (including this guide) as orientation, not authority.
